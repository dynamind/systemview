# The milk model in function machine terms. src/scene/machines.ts reads it and finds the
# gaps (node scripts/gaps.mjs). ?world=machines shows it, with the gaps on each output.
#
#   system X           a function machine: what goes in, what comes out
#   outside X          a black box we do not open: only its boundary is modeled
#   need X             what someone wants; its input says what it accepts
#   rule X             a tolerance someone wrote down, imposed upstream
#
#   in:  a, b from Y   inputs; "from Y" names the system whose output it is. Only a
#                      system that stands in no boundary says "from": a boundary routes
#                      the outputs of its machines to their inputs by name. When several
#                      inputs name the same system, each one gets its own instance of it.
#   starts with: a, b  inputs the machine needs only to start: a stock, such as a cow or
#                      a building. "in" names the flows that keep it running.
#   in:  a from somewhere  a source we do not expand. Any machine can say it. The input
#                      needs no supplier and does not cross the boundary it stands in.
#   out: a to somewhere    a sink we do not expand: the output needs no receiver.
#   out: a, b          outputs; + wanted, ~ unwanted, ? unknown
#   contains: A, B     the machines inside this system's boundary
#   limits: a into Y   for a rule: the flow it sets a tolerance on
#   how, why           free text
#
#   d: v               under an output: its value on dimension d
#   d: a > b           under an input: the values it accepts on dimension d, most
#                      desired first. Values that are not listed are refused.
#
# The dimensions are treatment, consistency, access, price, temperature, volume and
# amount. Each one is an ordered vocabulary, like "class X orderedBy field" in Ontology55.
# An input that does not name a dimension does not care about it.
#
# Values carry through. A machine gives only the values it changes; it takes the rest from
# its input of the same name. Pasteurizing makes raw milk pasteurized, and the milk stays as
# consistent as it was. So one machine can stand in many boundaries.
#
# Gaps are derived, not written. Each value an output gives is compared with the
# ranking of the input that receives it:
#   - the first value: no gap
#   - a lower value: a pain point for the owner of the receiver; lower is worse
#   - a value that is not listed, or no input at all: refused; the receiver fails
# The "# gaps:" comments below say what the engine should find, so the model can be
# checked by hand first.

need Dairy on the table
  for consumers
  in: milk
      treatment: pasteurized > raw
      consistency: consistent > varies
      access: every day nearby > every day at the farm > with dry spells
      price: low > medium > high
  why: People want milk on the table: safe to drink, the same every time, at a price they can pay, and there when they want it.
  # Some consumers rank raw first. They are a second need with their own ranking.

# ------------------------------------------------------------ three ways, competing for the need's input

system Cow and bucket
  how: a family keeps a cow and milks it by hand
  starts with: a cow, a bucket, housing
  in: feed, water, care
  out: + milk
         treatment: raw
         consistency: varies
         access: with dry spells
         price: high
       + calves
       ~ manure
       ~ methane
  # gaps at the need: pain points on treatment, consistency, access (last of three) and price (last of three)
  # Where the cow comes from is an input problem we leave open: an input that no system gives out.
  # ? price: high, because a family must buy and keep a whole cow.

system Farm shop
  how: buy milk at a farm nearby, pasteurized on the farm
  contains: Pasteurizing
  in: raw milk from Dairy farm
  out: + milk
         consistency: varies
         access: every day at the farm
         price: medium
  # The raw milk comes from one dairy farm. The shop pasteurizes it with the same machine as
  # the Dairy plant; the milk it sells takes "pasteurized" from that machine.
  # Some farms sell raw milk too. That serves the second need, of consumers who rank raw first.
  # gaps at the need: pain points on consistency, access and price
  # ? price: medium is a guess.

system Market
  how: buy dairy in a shop around the corner, any day
  in: packaged milk from Dairy plant
      treatment: pasteurized
      consistency: consistent
      volume: bulk
  out: + milk
         access: every day nearby
         price: medium
  # gaps at the need: a pain point on price. The shop adds a margin, but scale lowers the cost.

# ------------------------------------------------------------ the dairy farm

system Dairy farm
  how: a farm milks its herd for the dairy, not for its own customers
  why: Selling to a dairy is a different business from selling at the door.
  contains: Herd, Rearing, Cooling, Fields
  in: water, genetics, vet and contractors, cows and bulls from somewhere, feed
  out: + raw milk
       + calves
       + cows and bulls to somewhere
       ~ manure
       ~ runoff
       ~ methane
  # The outputs follow the order of the outputs of Herd. Each one gathers what the machines
  # inside leave over under its name: the cold raw milk from Cooling, the manure above the
  # limit, and the methane and runoff. Inputs that nothing inside makes come in.
  # The farm names feed as its own input: it buys feed, and it grows grass feed on its fields.
  # The two compete, and both go to the herd and the young stock.
  # The farm buys cows and bulls too. They go into the herd, next to its own young stock.
  # The trade in cattle between farms is not part of this model: the cattle come from
  # somewhere, and the cattle that the farm sells go to somewhere.
  # The farm names calves, and cows and bulls, as its own outputs: it sells some of them,
  # and keeps the rest for its herd. So they flow both inside and out. The culled cows and
  # bulls of the herd leave as cows and bulls too.
  # Beef is not an output of the farm. It comes from the butcher.

system Herd
  in: water, genetics, vet and contractors, cows and bulls, feed
  out: + raw milk
         treatment: raw
         temperature: warm
       + calves
       culled cows and bulls
       ~ manure
       ~ methane
  # The farmer improves the genetics of the herd by breeding: the calves of the best cows
  # grow up to replace the others. Culling starts early, more and more on genomics: calves
  # that the farm does not keep leave before rearing. A cow with persistent problems at
  # milking leaves the herd later.

system Rearing
  in: calves
  out: + cows and bulls
  # Opfok: the farm raises its calves into cows and bulls, for its own herd or for sale.

system Cooling
  in: raw milk
  out: + raw milk
         temperature: cold
  why: Raw milk lasts 7 to 10 days when it is cold, and about 2 hours when it is warm.

system Fields
  in: manure
      amount: within Deposition limit
  out: + grass feed
       ~ runoff

rule Deposition limit
  limits: manure into Fields
  why: It reduces the runoff into the groundwater. The manure above the limit leaves the farm.

rule Milk quota
  limits: raw milk out of Dairy farm
  why: From 1984 to 2015 the EU capped how much milk each farm could deliver.

# ------------------------------------------------------------ the dairy plant

system Dairy plant
  how: a cooperative plant collects the milk of many farms, mixes, pasteurizes and packs it, and brings it to the shops
  why: The market deals with a few plants instead of every farm. A plant closes that gap for many farms and many markets at once, so it pays for itself.
  contains: Mixing, Pasteurizing, Packing
  in: raw milk from many Dairy farm
      treatment: raw
      temperature: cold
  out: + packaged milk
       ?
  # The plant gives out the packaged milk that Packing makes.
  # The plant does not accept milk that a farm pasteurized itself: it controls quality from the raw milk on.
  # Pasteurized milk from a Farm shop would be refused here: "pasteurized" is not listed.
  # "from many": the input adds up the outputs of many suppliers. Bulk is not a quality of one
  # farm's milk; it comes from the sum. Draft syntax. A minimum, as in Ontology55's
  # "requires: k", could follow: "from at least 50 Dairy farm".

system Mixing
  in: raw milk
  out: + raw milk
         consistency: consistent
         volume: bulk

system Pasteurizing
  in: raw milk
  out: + milk
         treatment: pasteurized

system Packing
  in: milk, packaging from somewhere
  out: + packaged milk

# ------------------------------------------------------------ outside: boundaries only

outside Climate
  in: methane
      amount: low
  out: ~ global warming
  # Methane comes from Cow and bucket and from Herd. The engine routes each output
  # to the receiver that names it; an output with no receiver is fog.
  # The outputs do not name an amount yet, so the engine cannot find this gap. ? Add amount to methane.

outside Groundwater
  in: runoff
      amount: low
  # Nothing comes out until someone needs the groundwater.
